For general blocking, I use the `/etc/hosts` files from [someonewhocares.org/hosts/zero/](https://someonewhocares.org/hosts/zero/).

> **NOTE:** I saw on [Reddit](https://redlib.freedit.eu/r/webdev/comments/1sb6hzk/adobe_wrote_to_my_hosts_file_ive_never_had_an_app/) today that someone caught Adobe using their product installers to modify `/etc/hosts`, which is _extremely_ creepy. It might be a good idea to make `/etc/hosts` read-only (even for root) using `sudo chattr +i /etc/hosts`. This method is more powerful than just modifying the file's permissions and can only be undone by `sudo chattr -i /etc/hosts`. (This probably won't defeat a sophisticated installer that simply runs the `chattr` command itself, but it might be sufficient to block casual modification attempts.) It might also be a good idea to store a hash of the file somewhere safe and then compare `/etc/hosts` against the hash every once in a while to determine whether or not it's been modified.

For browser-based blocking, I use [uBlock Origin](https://addons.mozilla.org/en-US/firefox/addon/ublock-origin/) with the addition of a one-line Google login pop-up filter (added to "My Filters"): `||accounts.google.com/gsi/iframe`
